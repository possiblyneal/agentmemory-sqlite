# 2026-10-20 — BEAM retrieval-only (100K and 500K)

**Commit:** the commit that adds this file, on top of `a0de1e3`
**Bench:** BEAM (arXiv 2510.27246), HF `Mohammadta/BEAM`, CC-BY-SA-4.0 (fetched, never committed). Retrieval only: no answerer, no judge
**N:** dev split only, abstention skipped. 100K: 213 questions over 20 conversations. 500K: 377 questions over 35 conversations (lexical adapters), 80 questions over a 7-conversation sample (embedding adapter)
**K:** 15 primary, rescored at 5, 10 and 20 from the same retrieval
**Hardware:** Debian 13, 8 cores shared with other agents (load 10 to 35), Node 22.23.2
**Embeddings:** on-device `Xenova/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` for `agentmemory`; none for `agentmemory-bm25` and `grep`
**LLM:** none (no compression, no summaries)

Scores Recall on BEAM's probing questions, using each question's `source_chat_ids` as
gold. No product code changed; this is a baseline for the supersession slice.

## Headline

`agentmemory` finds the cited chat in its top 15 for **0.84** of the 100K questions (mean
gold recall **0.657**), against **0.75** (**0.528**) for `agentmemory-bm25` and **0.76**
(**0.527**) for `grep`. On the 500K sample it holds up: **0.76** hit, **0.619** recall,
against **0.66** and **0.489** for BM25 on the same 7 conversations.

The supersession slice is not where Recall is weak. At k=15 on 100K, `knowledge_update`
recall is **0.878** and `contradiction_resolution` **0.903** (hit **0.96** and **1.00**);
the cited turn is usually rank 1. What this does not show is whether the newer claim
outranks the older one: gold holds both, and recall counts both equally (see Notes).
The weak abilities are `event_ordering` (**0.296**) and `summarization` (**0.328**),
whose gold spans many turns, so top 15 cannot hold it.

## Why k=15

The paper's best retrieval budget is K=15, and K=20 degrades from noise (§5). Here the
retrieval unit is a Turn, not the paper's chunk, so the paper's number is a prior, not a
fit. The sweep below shows Recall still rising from 15 to 20 (100K: **0.657** to **0.700**),
so the noise the paper reports at K=20 (extra context hurting the answer) is something a
retrieval-only score cannot see. k=15 is kept as the primary because it is the largest
budget the paper found useful to an answerer, and the sweep lets a reader pick another.

## Methodology

- **Unit.** A Session is a Turn: a user message and the replies to it, id `t<first chat id>`.
  The ticket's "chat batches as sessions" was not used: a 100K conversation has 3 to 5
  batches and a 500K one has 10, so any k of 15 returns the whole chat and every ability
  scores 1.0. A chat id maps to the Turn holding it. Turns are dated by their batch's
  `time_anchor` at 09:00 plus a minute per Turn, so ingest order is chat order.
- **Gold.** `source_chat_ids` for every ability but abstention, flattened: a list, or a
  dict of lists for `knowledge_update` (`original_info`, `updated_info`),
  `contradiction_resolution` and `temporal_reasoning`. Recall is the share of gold Turns in
  the top k, so a multi-turn gold (`event_ordering`, `summarization`, up to 15 ids) caps below
  1.0 whatever the ranker does. The loader fails on a cited id absent from the chat.
- **Skipped.** Abstention (40 per 100K, 70 per 500K) and probes without ids (5 on 100K,
  1 on 500K); `probing_questions` is a Python repr, parsed by a small literal parser.
- **Split.** `selectSplit("beam", ..., id, ability)`, dev only, salt and share unchanged;
  the holdout was not opened. Dev is 60% of each ability's questions, drawn per question, so
  a conversation contributes about 10 of its 18 questions.
- **Ingest.** Each conversation is ingested once and its dev questions are answered from that
  sandbox (`/remember` per Turn, chunked, as the LongMemEval path does). The trailing
  `->-> 1,3` plan marker on messages is stripped.
- **Cost.** `agentmemory`, 100K: 2,866 Turns, 87 min of ingest (6.5 s a Turn at load 30,
  0.5 s at load 10), query p50 47 ms. 500K sample: 3,656 Turns, 31 min, query p50 59 ms.
  Full 500K for the embedding adapter is 19,029 Turns, about 2 to 3 hours, so a sample was
  run. The lexical adapters took 5 min (100K) and 43 min (500K, all 35 conversations, under load).
- **Adapters.** `grep` and `agentmemory-bm25` have no network or model needs. The `vector`
  adapter needs an OpenAI key and was not run.

## Reproduce

```sh
git checkout <sha>
npm ci && npm run build
# fetch the splits as eval/README.md "BEAM" describes, then:
export BEAM_PATH=/path/to/repo/tmp/datasets/beam
npm run eval:beam -- --size 100K --adapters grep,agentmemory-bm25 --out tmp/beam-runs/lex
npm run eval:beam -- --size 500K --adapters grep,agentmemory-bm25 --out tmp/beam-runs/lex
npm run eval:beam -- --size 100K --adapters agentmemory --out tmp/beam-runs/emb
npm run eval:beam -- --size 500K --adapters agentmemory --conversations 7 --out tmp/beam-runs/emb-sample7
```

## Notes

- **Gold holds both sides of a supersession.** For `knowledge_update` it is the original and
  the updated turn; for `contradiction_resolution` both statements. A top 15 that holds the
  stale turn but not the new one still scores 0.5. The 0.878 and 0.903 above therefore say the
  slice is findable, not that the newer claim wins; that needs the roles kept apart (updated
  turn in the top k, and ahead of the original), which this run did not score. It is the
  first step of #266.
- **Contradiction resolution is not near zero here.** The paper finds it near zero for every
  method because it scores an answer; Recall finds both statements (hit 1.00 on 100K), so
  the failure is in answering, not retrieving.
- **Unit size drives the weak abilities.** `event_ordering` and `summarization` cite 4 to 15
  turns spread across the chat; at k=15 they cannot reach full recall, and hit (any gold in
  the top 15) is the fairer read there (0.71 and 0.86).
- **Embeddings pay on the soft abilities.** `preference_following` (0.783 vs 0.500) and
  `instruction_following` (0.497 vs 0.333) gain most from the vector leg over BM25: their
  gold turn states a standing rule the question does not repeat. The cut in `knowledge_update`
  between adapters is small on 100K and unreliable on the 500K sample (n=7).
- **5x the haystack costs little recall.** BM25 goes from 0.528 (100K) to 0.499 (500K, all
  35 conversations); `agentmemory` goes from 0.657 to 0.619 on the sample, where BM25 reads
  0.489. The sample is too small to separate a scale effect from which conversations were
  drawn.
- **Not for `fast`.** 100K with the embedding adapter is 1.5 h to 3.5 h depending on load;
  it belongs in `full` only if #265 is fixed and the numbers discriminate.
